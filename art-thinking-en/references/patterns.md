### 1. Driven by how people feel (ease, fun), not by efficiency
- "How elegant the tech is, how far it beats other software on numbers — all of that comes after."
- The selling point of a tool for directing AI: the joy of directing, not the efficiency.
- A tool that is only about efficiency and correctness gets: "I don't know what it's for."
- **Use it**: when a project is only about efficiency, first find who it makes life easier for, or who gets to have fun.

### 2. The artist is the first tester
- "Wherever I get stuck is where the people I hand it to will get stuck."
- "As a human, I can't read its state."
- **Use it**: if they can't understand it, the design isn't there yet. It isn't their fault.

### 3. Make invisible state visible and touchable
- Tap a part directly on the work; what you adjust lights up.
- State shown by position and shape; brightness shows how well something fits.
- Required exception: every object keeps its name next to it. Less text is fine; names must stay.

### 4. Remove anything you must understand before using it
- No IP to type, no Start button, no English jargon.
- A board you can use without knowing any board game: no chess terms, no real chess pieces.
- "Users don't need to learn anything." "The point isn't having no interface."
- **Note**: what's removed is "having to learn first", not "being able to do it by hand."

### 5. Guide, don't restrict
- The usual things to reject are artificial limits: taking turns, distance having meaning, only dropping on lit spots, moving only along lines, a board that ends.
- "Traditional software has developers guess what users want. Hand that step back to the user and their AI."

### 6. A metaphor is a starting point, not a spec
- Games, chess, Go, the Big Bang, galaxies are all thinking tools.
- A version built literally from the metaphor is almost always wrong. What's wanted is the feel: fun, readable at a glance, alive.

### 7. Protect the detail, and keep the whole orderly
- Keep apart what a pipeline would merge, so subtle detail isn't eaten in conversion.
- "If the engine is ours, we can make it as fine as we want."
- "The whole system should be compact and clear at a glance." Freedom yes, but the picture must stay orderly.

### 8. It only counts when you see it move
- Done means it visibly moves, and you can check it on a phone first.
- Prototypes that move, recorded as videos, watched on a phone.

### 9. Make it exist first
- "As long as the thing gets made first, that's fine."
- Several rough prototype versions in one day beat one perfect plan.

### 10. The ideation loop
1. A sentence from outside, or a metaphor, sets off some "daydreaming."
2. A long stream of thoughts comes out by voice.
3. "Organize what I said and confirm whether it's right."
4. The AI gives one concrete proposal, ideally one that moves.
5. Instinctive "that's wrong" or "I can't read it", plus a new idea ("it just occurred to me…").
6. When stuck, write a handoff document and let another AI start fresh.
- Wants divergence ("things I couldn't even imagine"), but only one concrete proposal per round.

### 11. When someone else's tool limits the work, build your own
- A generic tool flattens the detail that made the work good.
- "The work performs inside other people's software, and is limited by it."
- **Use it**: check whether the tool in the middle eats detail or caps how the work can look.

### 12. If it can't be handed over, it doesn't count
- "The logic worked, but it was tied to my own setup and couldn't be given to anyone."
- "However good the work is, if the person receiving it can't use it, the delivery isn't done."

### 13. Build for yourself first, then for everyone; think big
- "Build it for ourselves first, then make it usable for everyone who receives it."
- Small team plus AI, aiming at what used to take a whole team.

### 14. Fun up front, rigor backstage
- Talk about fun and experiments, while keeping written decisions, performance budgets, clean resets behind the scenes.
- Reason: some moments, like a live show, can't be redone. The rigor is respect for the person on stage.
- **Risk**: rigor, done long enough, eats the fun. Check whether the exciting features still say "in progress."

### 15. An easy path for everyone, an open path for experts
- Experts use nodes; everyone else uses layers built from those nodes.
- Mainly conversation, but simple parameters remain adjustable by hand.

## In the artist's own voice

Use these to check tone and values.

- "I'm not making things for the sake of making them. I make them so the people who receive my work have an easy time — I pave the stretch of road that comes after I hand it over."
- "Tools are an extension of my work. I solve the trouble people would run into ahead of time, so what they see is what I actually made."
- "I want a person to look at it — whatever form it takes — and know that their next gesture can decide a lot of things."
- "There's no 'whose turn'… I do things while you do things; that's a smoother state."
- "It should still be able to go anywhere, but how brightly a place lights up shows where it fits better."
- "Some games never end."
- "I treat the AI as another player… I want a subtle 'you get me, I get you' state between us."
- "First organize what I said and confirm with me whether it's right. I don't know how this would turn out either — you design and arrange it for me."
